You can launch a Discord-like experience quickly if you focus on a few essential pieces first: a community home, a launch channel, and a small set of permissions.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create your first server">
    Start with one main server for your brand or community. Give it a clear name, add a profile banner, and choose a welcome channel where new members land.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Invite members">
    Share an invite link or onboarding code with your early users. Keep the first wave small so you can learn what they want and tune the channel layout.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set up channels">
    Create a few core channels such as **#welcome**, **#announcements**, **#support**, and **#general**. This keeps the app organized from day one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assign roles">
    Give admins, moderators, and members clear permissions. Start with a simple role model so people understand what they can do.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Launch a live room">
    Test voice chat, share a community update, and encourage your first conversation. Live rooms make the app feel active from the start.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Good defaults

- Keep one channel for announcements.
- Use one channel for support or questions.
- Create a place for off-topic conversations.
- Turn on member verification for new users if your community grows quickly.
