Servers are the heart of a Discord-style app. They give your community a shared home, while channels help members focus on the right conversations at the right time.

## Recommended structure

A healthy server usually has a small set of purpose-built channels:

- **Welcome** for new members and server rules
- **Announcements** for launches, updates, and important news
- **General** for casual conversation
- **Support** for product and account questions
- **Community** for events, user stories, or feedback

## Use categories to organize content

Categories make large servers easier to scan. For example, you could group channels like this:

- **Community**
  - #welcome
  - #announcements
  - #general
- **Support**
  - #help
  - #bug-reports
  - #feature-requests
- **Voice**
  - #lounge
  - #events
  - #off-topic

## Create clear rules

Set expectations early so members know where to post and what is appropriate. A good server guide includes:

- how to ask for help
- where to share feedback
- which channels are for announcements only
- how to use threads and direct messages

## Best practices

- Keep channel names short and obvious.
- Don’t create too many channels at launch.
- Pin important messages in your welcome or announcement channels.
- Archive or hide old channels when they are no longer useful.
